About Janvi
Daryanani

A property advisor's role is to understand capital goals, risk appetite, timing and portfolio alignment, not to push the hottest launch without context.

That said, there is one thing often misunderstood about this market's urgency. In most global cities, urgency is a sales tactic. In Dubai, it's a structural reality. You're not competing with one buyer; you're competing with global capital that moves 24/7 across time zones.

Here's what prepared investors understand. By the time you're still evaluating, hundreds of token payments are already placed. Early commitment isn't impulsive; it's strategic. It's how investors secure the best positioning, unit, quality and long-term upside. In this market, hesitation doesn't just cost money; it costs opportunity.

I've seen capable, well-funded clients lose prime units because they wanted to explore every option. When clarity finally came, the queue had moved, and the right asset was gone. Dubai doesn't reward hesitation; it rewards those who are prepared.

My advice to serious investors for 2026 is to have your capital ready, have your strategy defined, and have an advisor you trust before the launch date, because when you're finally sure, the best units are rarely still available.
